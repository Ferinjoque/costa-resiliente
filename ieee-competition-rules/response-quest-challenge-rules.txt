IEEE Response QuestTM Challenge
Official Rules
Updated: 13 April 2026
1.

Challenge: IEEE Response Quest Challenge 2026 (the “Challenge”) is a global competition in which IEEE
members collaborate across disciplines—including data science, GIS, emergency management, software
engineering, and related fields—to develop solutions that enable near‑real‑time situational awareness for
emergency managers and disaster responders during natural disasters. It seeks to integrate disparate data sources,
such as satellite imagery, radar, GIS, weather, census maps, road conditions, power outages, earthquake data,
and social media, into a unified, actionable interface. The scope is intentionally multidisciplinary, intersecting
disaster response, community resilience, public safety, and technology. Teams are encouraged to work in
cross‑disciplinary groups to strengthen the technical and operational relevance of their solutions. The Challenge
consists of three phases: (1) Registration, (2) Concept Submission, and (3) Product Submission. Entries will be
evaluated according to the criteria described herein.

7.

Submission Requirements: To submit an Entry, each Entrant must complete all required submission steps for
each phase of the Challenge as outlined herein.
Phase 1 (Registration): Each Entrant must submit a completed registration form through the official Challenge
platform. Registrations will be reviewed for eligibility and completeness, and approved Entrants will be permitted
to advance to Phase 2. Only the Entrant is required to accept these Rules during registration; additional team
members, if any, will be required to confirm their participation and acceptance of these Rules if the team is
advanced to Phase 2.
Phase 2 (Concept Submission): Each Entrant must submit a written description of their proposed solution using
the online form provided. Entrants may select any natural disaster scenario (e.g., hurricanes, wildfires, floods,
earthquakes, or other events) that meaningfully supports the goals of the Challenge. The concept must describe
the disaster scenario the Entrant intends to address and the Entrant’s high‑level approach to using, integrating,
and presenting data to support emergency managers and disaster responders. Phase 2 Entries will be evaluated
according to the Challenge rubric, including the Entrant’s articulation of timeliness and real‑time responsiveness,
the comprehensiveness and appropriateness of the data they intend to use, the quality of their planned integration
and responsible data‑handling approach, the clarity and operational readiness of the proposed interface, and the
degree of scenario fit, insightfulness, innovation, and technical or data creativity demonstrated in the concept.
Only those Entries selected by the selection Committee (defined below) will advance to Phase 3.
All team members listed by the Entrant in the Phase 2 submission form must individually accept these Challenge
Rules within the deadline set by IEEE. All listed team members must complete the acceptance process. If any
listed team member does not complete the process, the entire team submission will be disqualified.
Phase 3 (Product Submission): Each Entrant must submit a working demonstration of their concept, including
a two‑to‑five‑minute video showing a real person using or interacting with the product in a manner that enables
judges to understand how the interface functions in practice. The video must clearly demonstrate the core
functionality of the product. Phase 3 Entries must demonstrate the Entrant’s ability to implement their proposed
approach in a manner consistent with the Challenge rubric, including the product’s timeliness and real‑time
responsiveness, its use of relevant and appropriate data, the quality and reliability of its data integration and
synthesis, the usability and operational readiness of the interface for emergency responders, and the degree to
which the final product aligns with the chosen scenario and exhibits insightfulness, innovation, and technical or
data creativity. Optional supporting materials may be included at the Entrant’s discretion.
A select group of finalists may be invited to present their solution remotely, and participation in such a presentation
shall be required if requested by the Committee. Any remote presentation will be used to assist the judges in their

be disqualified at the Sponsor’s sole discretion.
8.

Challenge Prize: The Challenge will award prizes from a total prize pool of USD $100,000 (each, a “Prize”).
Prizes will be distributed across four primary award categories: First Place (USD $30,000), Second Place (USD
$20,000), Third Place (USD $15,000), and a Judge’s Choice Award (USD $10,000). The Judge’s Choice Award
will be selected qualitatively by the Committee and granted to the team that demonstrates excellence in a manner
not fully captured by the scoring rubric. In addition to these awards, the Committee may, in its sole discretion,
grant one or more Honorary Mention cash prizes, up to a combined total of USD $25,000, in recognition of
Entries that exhibit exceptional performance on one or more evaluation criteria, address challenging use cases,
or demonstrate notable innovation. The awarding of any Prize is contingent upon the team’s full compliance
with these Rules, including the timely completion of all required documentation. Prizes may consist of monetary
awards, recognition, or other forms of acknowledgment as determined by the Sponsor in its sole discretion.
For winning teams, each team member will receive an equal portion of the applicable prize funds.

9.

Judging Criteria: A selection committee (the “Committee”) composed of qualified judges with expertise in
disaster response, data science, GIS, emergency management, software engineering, and related fields will
evaluate all Entries. Entries in both Phase 2 (Concept Submission) and Phase 3 (Product Submission) will be
assessed according to five rubric categories: (1) Timeliness, Real‑Time Responsiveness & Technical Reliability;
(2) Comprehensiveness, Use of Available Data & Novel Data Discovery; (3) Integration, Synthesis Quality &
Responsible Data Handling; (4) Usability, Clarity & Operational Readiness for Emergency Responders; and (5)
Scenario Fit, Insightfulness, Innovation & Technical/Data Creativity. These criteria reflect the core goals of the
Challenge and will be applied consistently across both phases, with expectations increasing as Entrants progress
from concept to working prototype. The Committee will employ a standardized scoring system to ensure fairness
and objectivity, and will evaluate each Entry based on its technical soundness, operational relevance, data quality,
user‑centered design, and overall merit as defined by the rubric. The Committee will select the winning teams
based on the highest overall evaluation in accordance with these criteria.

10. Selection of Winning Entrant: The Committee will review all eligible Entries and determine, in its sole
discretion, which Entries best meet the standards as defined by the Challenge rubric. The Committee may, but is
not required to, conduct additional review, request clarification from Entrants, or engage in deliberation to ensure
that the selected Winner(s) reflect the intent and objectives of the Challenge. Following completion of the judging
process, the Committee will select the Winners based on the highest overall evaluation in accordance with the
judging criteria described herein. The number of Winners, the distribution of any Prizes, and any distinctions
among Winners shall be determined solely by the Sponsor based on the Committee’s evaluations. All decisions
of the Sponsor are final and binding.
